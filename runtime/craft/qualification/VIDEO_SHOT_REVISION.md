# Editable Video shot revision and native suite

## Result

On 18 November 2026, A dope CLI produced an original portrait film suite using
Vector, Motion, Audio and Video through installed headless CLI workers. The
15-second original became a 17-second revision with a changed ending animation,
changed final caption and a two-second final-pose hold. The delivery contains
nine portable editable projects, both films, a further portable replacement,
an original audio input, an asset-binding recipe and an offline HTML preview.

The software change adds typed picture-source replacement to `video.edit`.
Before this change, the installed adapter rejected that edit type. It could
change captions, trims and audio gain but could not perform the ending-content
revision from the reference workflow without rebuilding the timeline.

This closes that operation gap and provides concrete evidence for the plan's
first Video and Effects targets. It is a deterministic abstract study at
360 × 640 and 24 fps, not photorealistic generation, a supplied-logo campaign,
owner aesthetic approval, hosted qualification or complete application/release
acceptance. The mark and tone bed are original qualification assets. No paid
image/video model, remote action, download or global service change was used.

## Production chain and editability

Vector created a transparent cobalt three-bar mark with retained native shapes,
SVG and PNG. Three Motion projects used that exact PNG as a media layer and
cream geometric shapes over charcoal. Each original shot occupies five seconds
in the Video timeline. The final source has seven seconds of media: animation
ends at frame 119, leaving the final pose available through frame 167.

An original 17-second stereo tone fixture was imported into Audio. Native Audio
retained the editable mix, gain and fade settings and exported the bed for Video.
Video assembled the three sources, two centered 12-frame dissolves, the audio
bed and three native Inter Regular captions. All successful Video jobs require
the previously qualified native glyph diagnostics.

The ending revision changed two rotation tracks and one scale track in Motion.
Independent native JSON comparison confirmed that every field outside those
three property values and keyframes remained unchanged. Layer IDs and the
editable mapping stayed byte-identical. Video then replaced only the ending
source, changed its caption and extended the ending, caption and audio by
48 frames. Independent native comparison confirmed that only that source item,
caption string and those three durations changed. Every other clip field,
sequence setting and transition remained exact.

Video retains rendered source clips; the original Motion documents provide
editable animation. The nine-project delivery makes this boundary explicit.
Its recipe preserves reusable settings and points to local source artifacts.
A coding assistant must import those files and bind the returned project asset
IDs before submitting the typed parameters. It is not an immediately executable
shell script or an unbound native request.

## Verification

The installed run completed ten successful native jobs and six expected failed
native jobs. One foreign-project asset request was rejected during submission
and created no job. All sixteen recorded native execution process groups were
absent afterward. The job and installation sequence passed 32 checks.

Independent media/native oracles passed another 25 checks:

- Full decodes contained exactly 360 and 408 frames for the 15- and 17-second
  films at 24 fps. The further portable replacement also decoded all 408 frames.
- All decoded frames before frame 234 were pixel-identical between original and
  revised films. The second centered dissolve begins at frame 234, so its final
  six frames before the cut correctly incorporate the changed incoming shot.
  The first two source assets remain byte-identical throughout.
- Repeating the same authorized replacement produced identical decoded pixels
  for all 408 frames. Already-retained media was reused without overwriting it.
- During the final two-second hold, the largest mean RGB difference from the
  first held frame was 0.04282 on a 0–255 scale, below the declared tolerance of
  one mean RGB level for the lossy H.264 output.
- At the first dissolve's center, mean picture difference from the incoming
  source was 25.42066 RGB levels. After the dissolve it was 0.05978. The native
  transition records independently show frames 114–125 and 234–245.
- Independent probing confirmed portrait dimensions, 24/1 fps, exact picture
  durations and stereo 48 kHz audio within one video frame of each duration.
  Full decoded audio was finite, non-silent and unclipped, with decoded lengths
  within two AAC blocks of the expected sample count. This is signal evidence,
  not human music approval.
- Importing the revised portable project into fresh state allowed another
  picture replacement and caption revision. Native identities and other fields
  remained exact. The restored original ending geometry differed by only
  0.05593 mean RGB levels after encoding.
- Every inspected original/revised checkout continued to match its complete
  artifact inventory after later work.

The six failed native jobs covered title and audio targets, a source too short
for the slot, audio supplied as picture, extension beyond available media and
corrupt media. None published a result or changed the accepted project head.
The attempted overextension was clamped by the native engine and then rejected
by requested-trim verification; it was not silently accepted.

Nine local filesystem/schema regression tests passed. They cover exact retained
file reuse, default exclusive creation, different bytes of the same size,
project authorization, symlinks, hardlinks, FIFOs, missing authoritative blobs,
unsafe asset values and duplicate replacement targets. The FIFO case confirms
that inspection does not wait for a writer. Unchanged content is checked against
the project's authoritative asset record rather than its filename or receipt.

The first negative harness assumed a foreign asset would become a failed job.
The actual API rejected it earlier, during submission. The original failure log
is retained. A continuation verified the absence of that job and completed the
portable case without rerunning or suppressing the six native failures.

## Installation and release boundary

The qualification candidate is `0.1.0-video-replacement-qualification`. It retains
all seventeen native engine families and the previously built Video engine from
pinned FilmCraft source. This unit changes the maintained Python adapter and
asset-materialization path; it does not rebuild or repin the native engine.
The installed adapter, worker and store exactly matched their maintained files.
The native payload seal verified before and after the job sequence.

The private installation is an ad-hoc rehearsal with no installed service,
publisher authentication or notarization. The retained machine-local
`video-shot-revision` checkpoint identifies exact package hashes, source hashes,
all job requests/results, independent oracle data, source/output inventories,
visual inspection, regression checks, repository verification, measurement and
local commit. Generated fixture assets do not enter the public source export.

Full-product requirements remain: broader per-application contracts and the
accepted branded campaign, Motion glyph qualification, Linux/Cloud execution
and portability, dependency notice completion, signing and stock-host tests,
and real billing/provider qualification under the owner's remote-action rules.
The shipped source changes and local suite are progress toward that full scope.
